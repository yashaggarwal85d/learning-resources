# Fact-check — LSM compaction and amplification

## Claims table

| # | Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Leveled compaction merges `Ln-1` into `Ln`; non-overlapping runs; exponentially increasing per-level target sizes | Confirmed | `sources/rocksdb-compaction.txt`: "Compaction into level N (Ln) merges data from Ln-1 into Ln."; `sources/rocksdb-leveled.txt`: "All non-0 levels have target sizes... | 2 | Tiered compaction accumulates several similar-sized runs, merges them together only once a threshold is reached | Confirmed | `sources/rocksdb-compaction.txt`: "A common approach for tiered is to merge sorted runs of similar size, without having the notion of levels"; `sources/rocksdb-universal.txt`: "tiered waits for several sorted runs with similar size and merge them together" |
| 3 | Leveled minimizes space amplification at the cost of read+write; tiered minimizes write at the cost of read+space; hybrid lands between both | Confirmed | `sources/rocksdb-compaction.txt`: "Classic Leveled compaction... minimizes space amplification at the cost of read and write amplification."; "Tiered compaction minimizes write amplification at the cost of read and space amplification."; "Tiered+Leveled has less write amplification than leveled and less space amplification than tiered." |
| 4 | A size-amplification-ratio formula: sum of non-largest-run sizes over the largest run's size, 0 = ideal | Confirmed | `sources/rocksdb-universal.txt`: "size amplification ratio = (size(R1) + size(R2) + ... size(Rn-1)) / size(Rn)"; "0 is the perfect size amplification" |
| 5 | Monkey: a uniform per-level filter false-positive rate is suboptimal; worst-case lookup cost is proportional to the sum of all levels' false-positive rates | Confirmed | `sources/monkey.txt`: "existing designs assign the same false positive rate... regardless of the size of the run"; "the worst-case point lookup cost over the whole LSM-tree is proportional to the sum of the false positive rates of all filters" |

## Red team

1. **Does the RUM conjecture's own "read/update/memory" map cleanly onto this topic's "read/write/space" amplification?** Not exactly, and the outline and `coverage.json` are careful to say so: the RUM paper's own three terms are *overheads* of a general access method (read overhead, update overhead, memory/storage overhead), while this topic's three measured numbers are *amplification ratios* specific to LSM-tree compaction (write amplification, read amplification, space amplification). The reel names the RUM conjecture as the generalization this chapter's own three measured numbers are an instance of, and never claims the paper itself uses "write amplification" or "space amplification" as its own vocabulary — `SOURCES.md`'s own "Contents" note for the RUM source states this explicitly.
2. **Is "no policy minimizes all three" actually proven, or just asserted from the RocksDB doc's own trade-off language?** The RocksDB source states the trade-off as an empirical/design fact about leveled vs. tiered vs. hybrid specifically; the RUM paper's own conjecture is the general argument for why no access method can. The reel's `TradeoffReveal` step draws on the RocksDB-specific trade-off (three concrete, named policies), and `ProveIt`/post-notes separately cite the RUM conjecture as the general principle — these are kept as two distinct, correctly-attributed claims, not merged into one over-claim.
3. **Does Monkey's result contradict or compete with leveled/tiered compaction?** No — checked directly against the paper: Monkey tunes Bloom-filter memory allocation *within* an existing merge policy (any of leveled, tiered, or a hybrid), it does not propose a new merge policy itself. The reel's `MonkeyReveal` step is worded to make this an orthogonal, independent knob, not a fourth compaction family.
4. **Does Dostoevsky's Lazy Leveling claim to beat the RUM trade-off (i.e., minimize all three amplifications at once)?** No — checked directly: the paper's own claim is that Lazy Leveling "maintains the same bounds" on lookup cost and space amplification while improving updates, and Dostoevsky "navigates" a space of trade-offs under "a user constraint on space-amplification," i.e., it still accepts a trade, just finds a better point on it per workload. The reel's `DostoevskyReveal` and takeaway explicitly say "there is still no setting that minimizes read, write, and space amplification together," matching the paper's own framing.
5. **Predict-step distractor check**: step 2 (`CompactionPredict`)'s wrong options were checked against what the sources actually rule out — "nothing, disk is cheap" is ruled out because every source frames unbounded run growth as a read-cost problem, not a non-problem; "merge every run into one single run on every flush" is ruled out because it's exactly what both leveled and tiered avoid, for cost reasons stated in the RocksDB source; "delete the oldest runs" is ruled out because a run can hold live, non-obsolete data at any age, per topic 28's own newest-wins mechanic. Step 6 (`TradeoffPredict`)'s wrong options were checked against the RUM conjecture's own statement and the RocksDB trade-off language directly, as in red-team point 2 above.
6. **Immutability/consistency check with topic 28**: this topic's own `examples.py` reuses topic 28's WAL/memtable/SSTable shape rather than inventing a new one, so a run entering compaction here is exactly the kind of run topic 28 flushes — checked by inspection of the two files' class shapes.
