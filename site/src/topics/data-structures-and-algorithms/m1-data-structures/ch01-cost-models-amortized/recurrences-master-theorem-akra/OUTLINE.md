## Learning outcomes

1. Draw the recursion tree of a divide-and-conquer recurrence, read each level's total off it, and say which end of the column dominates.
2. Apply the Master theorem in its three cases, including the regularity condition for case 3, and recognise a recurrence it does not cover (a gap between cases, a non-constant number of subproblems, a full-history recurrence).
3. Solve an unequal-split recurrence with Akra–Bazzi: find p from Σ aᵢ·bᵢᵖ = 1 (numerically when needed), evaluate the integral for f(n) = n, and see why deterministic selection is linear.
4. Prove a guessed bound by substitution with explicit constants, avoiding the drifting-constant false proof, and strengthen a right guess that fails by subtracting a lower-order term.

**The problem (opened in step 1, closed in step 12):** three recursive procedures on the chapter's vector of a million 64-bit keys, each handing over its step count as a recurrence: merge sort it (2T(n/2) + n), binary-search it (T(n/2) + 1), find its median by median of medians (T(n/5) + T(7n/10) + n). The second and third recurse differently from the first, yet one of the two double recursions is linear and the other is not. By step 12 all three are read off their trees, with their counts at n = 1,000,000 computed exactly.

9. **Akra–Bazzi: the exponent p** (`ExponentP`, h 380, duration 4.6).
