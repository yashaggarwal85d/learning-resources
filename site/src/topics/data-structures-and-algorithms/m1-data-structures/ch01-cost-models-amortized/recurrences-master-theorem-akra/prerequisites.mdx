### <span class="n">01</span> Asymptotic notation and the cost of a loop

You can read Θ, O and Ω, you know that constants and lower-order terms are dropped, and you have met the quicksort recurrence that the first reel solved by differencing. This reel counts the cost of a recursive procedure, so it needs the language the costs are written in.

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> is 3n² + 10n in Θ(n²), and is n log n in o(n²)? (Answer: yes to both; the first ratio tends to 3, the second to 0.)</p>

### <span class="n">02</span> The word-RAM: what a step costs

You know what one unit of cost is: a single operation on a machine word, with constant-time memory access. The reel counts word operations and drops the constants, and the Deep dive reuses the van Emde Boas halving (a 64-bit key's bits halved six times) from that topic.

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> on the word-RAM, what does one step cost, and why is adding two numbers a single step? (Answer: one unit; the numbers fit in a machine word.)</p>

### <span class="n">03</span> Proof by induction and geometric series

Induction: prove a statement for the base cases, then show that if it holds for smaller sizes it holds for this one. Geometric series: a sum whose terms shrink (or grow) by a constant factor is dominated by its largest term.

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> what do 1 + ½ + ¼ + … and 1 + 9/10 + 81/100 + … add up to? (Answer: 2, and 10; the sum of a shrinking series is 1/(1 − ratio).)</p>

<section class="prereq" data-section="procedures">

### <span class="n">04</span> Merge sort and binary search as procedures

You need to know what each does, not its analysis: merge sort splits the keys in two, sorts each half and merges; binary search compares with the middle key and keeps one half. The reel derives their step counts. Their own chapters come later.

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> how many probes does binary search make on 1,000,000 sorted keys in the worst case? (Answer: 20, which is ⌊log₂ 1,000,000⌋ + 1; the number of halvings is 19.)</p>
