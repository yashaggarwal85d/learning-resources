<section class="prereq" data-section="amortized">

### <span class="n">01</span> Aggregate and accounting methods

You know that an amortized bound is a worst-case bound on the total of a whole sequence divided by its length, never an average over random inputs. You have seen the doubling vector, the bank of coins that must stay at zero or above after every operation, the binary counter and the multipop stack, and the charge of 3 per append (4 for a growth factor of 1.5).

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> with a charge of 3 per append, what is the bank after 16 appends from empty? (Answer: 17, since 48 is charged and 31 is spent.)</p>

### <span class="n">02</span> Lower bounds and adversary arguments

You know that an adversary may choose the input that makes an algorithm pay the most. Here the adversary chooses the sequence of operations.

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> why is a bound proved for every sequence stronger than one proved for a random sequence? (Answer: it holds even for the sequence an adversary picks to be the worst.)</p>

<section class="prereq" data-section="stacks">

### <span class="n">03</span> Stacks

You know a stack holds items last in, first out: push adds on top, pop removes the top.

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> push a, push b, push c, then pop twice. What is left? (Answer: a.)</p>

</section>

<section class="prereq" data-section="binary">

### <span class="n">04</span> Binary numbers

You can count in binary: adding 1 turns the trailing 1s into 0s and the next 0 into a 1.

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> how many 1 bits does 10111 have, and how many bits change when you add 1? (Answer: four 1 bits; four bits change, 10111 becomes 11000.)</p>

</section>

<section class="prereq" data-section="logarithms">

### <span class="n">05</span> Logarithms

You can read log₂ of a size: the number of times you halve it to reach 1. The splay-tree potential on the later-structures step is a sum of such logarithms.

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> what is log₂ 1,024? (Answer: 10.)</p>

</section>
