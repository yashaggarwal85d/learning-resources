- S. Sattolo, "An algorithm to generate a random cyclic permutation", *Information Processing Letters* 22(6), 1986, pp. 315–317 — the single-cycle permutation used to defeat prefetching. [doi:10.1016/0020-0190(86)90073-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190%2886%2990073-6)
- R. H. Saavedra and A. J. Smith, "Measuring cache and TLB performance and their effect on benchmark runtimes", *IEEE Transactions on Computers* 44(10), 1995 — the strided method for cache-line size and TLB behaviour. [doi:10.1109/12.467697](https://doi.org/10.1109/12.467697)
- U. Drepper, "What Every Programmer Should Know About Memory", 2007 — framing and order-of-magnitude latency figures (numbers are dated). [PDF](https://www.akkadia.org/drepper/cpumemory.pdf)
- A. Aggarwal and J. S. Vitter, "The Input/Output Complexity of Sorting and Related Problems", *CACM* 31(9), 1988 — the external-memory model (M, B) the staircase is measured against. [doi:10.1145/48529.48535](https://doi.org/10.1145/48529.48535)
- M. Frigo, C. E. Leiserson, H. Prokop and S. Ramachandran, "Cache-Oblivious Algorithms", FOCS 1999 — the ideal-cache model and multilevel optimality. [doi:10.1109/SFFCS.1999.814600](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFFCS.1999.814600)
- Intel 64 and IA-32 Architectures Optimization Reference Manual, Volume 1 (current edition, chapter 3 "Hardware Prefetching for First-Level Data Cache" and chapter 9's prefetch sections) — the hardware prefetchers (streamer, spatial/adjacent-line, IP-stride) and their effect on a stride sweep. [PDF](https://cdrdv2.intel.com/v1/dl/getContent/814198?fileName=248966-Optimization-Reference-Manual-V1-049.pdf), not the general developer landing page.
- A. Fog, "The microarchitecture of Intel, AMD and VIA CPUs" (current edition) — independent documentation of cache and prefetcher behaviour. [PDF](https://www.agner.org/optimize/microarchitecture.pdf)
- The Linux Kernel documentation, "HugeTLB Pages" and "Transparent Hugepage Support" — x86 CPUs "normally support 4K and 2M (1G if architecturally supported) page sizes," with valid and default huge-page sizes stated as architecture- and configuration-dependent, not a single fixed 2 MiB default; and the effect of page size on TLB reach. [kernel.org](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/mm/hugetlbpage.html)
- P. Norvig, "Teach Yourself Programming in Ten Years" — the widely used latency-table orders of magnitude (DRAM ≈ 100 ns). [norvig.com](https://norvig.com/21-days.html)
