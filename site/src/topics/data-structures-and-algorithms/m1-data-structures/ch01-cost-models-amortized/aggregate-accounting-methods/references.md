Last verified against these sources: 12 October 2026, with every example run in `examples.py`. Fact-check record: `FACT-CHECK.md`. Expert sign-off: pending.

- Jeff Erickson, [Algorithms, Lecture 9: Amortized Analysis](https://jeffe.cs.illinois.edu/teaching/algorithms/notes/09-amortize.pdf) (Fa'13 notes, © 2018, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): the summation (aggregate), accounting (taxation) and charging methods, the binary counter, the double counter, Gray codes, and the exercises cited above.
- Avrim Blum, [15-451 Algorithms, Lecture 7: Amortized Analysis](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~avrim/451f11/lectures/lect0922.pdf) (CMU, Fall 2011): Definition 7.1, the doubling array by aggregate and by accounting, the counter, the weighted counter and the seed. Its footnote on charging pops is not used (see "The doubling vector, both ways").
- Sedgewick and Wayne, [Bags, Queues, and Stacks](https://algs4.cs.princeton.edu/13stacks/) (Algorithms, 4th ed., booksite §1.3): the resizing-array stack and the exercise on a queue from two stacks.
- Chris Okasaki, [Purely Functional Data Structures](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rwh/students/okasaki.pdf) (PhD thesis, CMU-CS-96-177, 1996): the banker's and physicist's methods as credited to Tarjan, and why they break under persistence.
- Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest and Stein, *Introduction to Algorithms*, 4th ed. (MIT Press, 2022), the chapter on amortized analysis: cited for framing only; it was not opened for this topic and no chapter number is claimed.
