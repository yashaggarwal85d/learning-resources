# FACT-CHECK: Local search and metaheuristics

Self-review as adversarial fact-checker, 2026-11-05.

| # | Claim | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Local search moves from solution to solution by local changes until stuck at a local optimum. | Confirmed | `sources/local-search.txt`: "Local search algorithms move from solution to solution in the space of candidate solutions... until a solution deemed optimal is found" and "When no improving neighbors are present, local search is stuck at a locally optimal point." |
| 2 | 2-opt was first proposed by Croes in 1958, with the basic move suggested earlier by Flood. | Confirmed | `sources/2-opt.txt`: "The 2-opt algorithm was first proposed by Croes in 1958,[1] although the basic move had already been suggested by Flood.[2]" |
| 3 | 2-opt removes two edges and adds two different edges, reconnecting the tour. | Confirmed | `sources/2-opt.txt`: "Since a 2-opt operation involves removing 2 edges and adding 2 different edges..." |
| 4 | On a twelve-point clustered instance, 2-opt from a random start reaches the true optimum (found by exact dynamic programming) less than half the time. | Confirmed by direct computation | `examples.py`: exact optimum via Held-Karp DP is 344.19; over 100 random-start trials, 2-opt reaches it exactly 45% of the time, with a worst case of 347.12. |
| 5 | Simulated annealing's acceptance rule (Kirkpatrick et al. form) accepts an improving move with probability 1, and a worsening move with probability exp(-(e'-e)/T). | Confirmed | `sources/simulated-annealing.txt`: "the acceptance probability function P(e, e', T) was defined as 1 if e' < e, and \exp(-(e'-e)/T) otherwise." |
| 6 | Simulated annealing was used by Kirkpatrick, Gelatt and Vecchi in 1983 for the traveling salesman problem, and the technique was introduced independently by others including Černý in 1985. | Confirmed | `sources/simulated-annealing.txt`: "In 1983, this approach was used by Kirkpatrick, Gelatt Jr., and Vecchi[6] for a solution of the traveling salesman problem. They also proposed its current name..." and "...Kirkpatrick, Gelatt and Vecchi (1983), and Cerny (1985)." |
| 7 | On the same twelve-point instance, simulated annealing reaches the true optimum far more often than plain 2-opt, and a genetic algorithm (this topic's own implementation) does not outperform plain 2-opt. | Confirmed by direct computation | `examples.py`: simulated annealing reaches the optimum 97% of 100 trials (mean 344.28) against 2-opt's 45% (mean 345.80); this topic's genetic algorithm reaches it only 43% of the time, with a worse mean (355.18) and a worse worst case (418.51) than 2-opt. |
| 8 | A genetic algorithm is a population of candidate solutions evolved by selection, crossover and mutation. | Confirmed | `sources/genetic-algorithm.txt`: "a population of candidate solutions... is evolved toward better solutions" and "a combination of genetic operators: crossover (also called recombination), and mutation." |
| 9 | PostgreSQL's GEQO treats query optimization as the traveling salesman problem, encoding a join tree as an integer string such as '4-1-3-2', using a steady-state GA with edge recombination crossover and deprecated mutation. | Confirmed | `sources/geqo-pg-intro.txt`: "The GEQO module approaches the query optimization problem as though it were the well-known traveling salesman problem (TSP)," the '4-1-3-2' example, "Usage of a steady state GA...", "Usage of edge recombination crossover...", and "Mutation as genetic operator is deprecated so that no repair mechanisms are needed to generate legal TSP tours." |

## Red team

- **Could the 45%/97%/43% success rates depend on the random seeds chosen?** The seeds (3 for the instance, 11/21/31 for the three trial runs) are fixed and recorded in `examples.py`; re-running with different seeds would shift the exact percentages but the qualitative finding — 2-opt is not reliable, annealing does better here, this particular genetic algorithm does not — was checked to hold across several seed choices during development (not committed, confirmed during this fact-check pass with seeds shifted by +100 on all three).
- **Does the genetic algorithm's weaker showing unfairly misrepresent genetic algorithms in general, or PostgreSQL's GEQO specifically?** No claim is made about genetic algorithms generally or about GEQO's own measured performance; `OUTLINE.md` → "Changes from the chapter plan" states explicitly that this topic's genetic algorithm is the builder's own simple implementation on a TSP tour, not a reproduction of GEQO (which operates on join-order trees with its own fitness function, the real planner's cost estimate). The topic's point is narrower and defensible: no fixed hierarchy among these three searches holds in general, which this instance demonstrates without overclaiming about any one family.
- **Near-miss checked:** confusing "simulated annealing always beats local search" (not a claim made anywhere in this topic) with "simulated annealing beat 2-opt on this one measured instance" (the actual, narrower claim) is the easiest error here — the topic states results as measured on one specific instance with one specific algorithm configuration throughout, never as a general ranking.
- **Could `geqo_threshold`'s default value have changed between PostgreSQL versions?** The quote is taken from "PostgreSQL 18 Documentation" (the "current" docs as of 2026-11-05); the topic does not claim this value holds for all past or future PostgreSQL versions, only for the documentation read.
