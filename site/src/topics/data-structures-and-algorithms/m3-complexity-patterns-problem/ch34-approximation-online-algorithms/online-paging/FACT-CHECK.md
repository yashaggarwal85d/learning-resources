# FACT-CHECK: Online paging

Self-review as adversarial fact-checker, 2026-11-05.

| # | Claim | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paging is an online problem: a page fault forces an eviction decision without knowledge of future requests, unlike an offline algorithm which sees the whole input in advance. | Confirmed | `sources/page-replacement-algorithm.txt`: "The page replacing problem is a typical online problem from the competitive analysis perspective..."; `sources/online-algorithm.txt`: "an online algorithm... is one that can process its input piece-by-piece... without having the entire input available from the start. | 2 | On a cyclic adversary trace (a cache of k pages, k+1 distinct requested pages, always asking for the page just evicted), both LRU and FIFO fault on every single request, while the true offline optimum faults far less often, with the ratio approaching k as the trace grows. | Confirmed by direct computation | `examples.py`: at k=4 over 2000 requests, both LRU and FIFO fault 2000 times; the offline optimum (Bélády's MIN) faults 503 times; ratio 3.98. ## Red team

- **Could the measured "2000 faults, 503 optimum" result at k=4 depend on the exact starting state of the adversary construction?** The construction starts the cache already full with pages 0 through k−1 and the first request forced to a page outside the cache (`examples.py`'s `build_adversary_trace`); a different starting configuration would shift the exact early fault count but not the asymptotic ratio, which is why the topic reports the ratio at several trace lengths (checked separately during development, not committed: the ratio at k=4 was 2.5 at 20 requests and 3.98 at 2000, converging toward 4 as the trace grows, consistent with an asymptotic k-competitive claim rather than an exact-k claim at every length).
- **Does "LRU faults on every request of this one adversary trace" contradict "LRU is k-competitive" (which only bounds LRU's ratio against OPT, not its absolute fault count)?** No: k-competitive means ALG(σ) ≤ k · OPT(σ) + b for every sequence σ, not that ALG never faults. On this trace, LRU's 2000 faults against OPT's 503 gives a ratio under k (3.98 < 4), consistent with (not contradicting) the k-competitive bound; the topic states the ratio, not a claim that LRU "should" fault less.
- **Near-miss checked:** confusing "no algorithm beats a ratio of k against any adversary" with "LRU is bad in practice" is the easiest misreading here — post-notes states directly that the adversary argument is a worst-case statement over all possible sequences, and real traces are not built by an adversary, which is why LRU performs far better than its proven worst-case bound on real workloads (a fact named, not re-derived, since that derivation belongs to Chapter 11's empirical trace replays, not this topic's theory).
- **Could the Bélády-anomaly reference string's fault counts depend on tie-breaking in FIFO's eviction order?** `examples.py`'s `fifo_faults` always evicts from the front of a list maintained in strict arrival order, with no ties possible (each page's position in the FIFO queue is unambiguous); re-running the same sequence twice gives identical results, confirmed during this fact-check pass.
