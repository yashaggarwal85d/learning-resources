- **Page fault**: a request for something not currently held in a limited cache or memory. *Where you meet it:* the single event every algorithm in this topic is trying to minimize the count of.
- **Eviction**: removing something from the cache to make room for what was just requested. *Where you meet it:* the one decision every policy in this topic has to make, and the only thing that differs between them.
- **Online algorithm**: one that must decide using only what has already happened, with no knowledge of what comes next. *Where you meet it:* every real cache policy, by necessity.
- **Offline algorithm**: one that sees the entire input in advance before making any decision. *Where you meet it:* the unimplementable benchmark this topic measures everything else against.
- **Adversary**: a hypothetical, deliberately hostile source of requests, built specifically to make a given algorithm look as bad as its proof allows. *Where you meet it:* the construction that proves no deterministic cache policy can promise better than a fixed worst-case ratio.
- **Worst case versus expected value**: a worst-case bound holds on every possible input, however unlikely; an expected value is an average over a stated distribution of inputs. *Where you meet it:* the gap this topic names between a proven worst-case ratio and a real cache's actual day-to-day behavior.
- **Harmonic number** (one line): the sum of one plus one half plus one third and so on, up to some n. *Where you meet it:* named once, as the building block of a result this topic describes but does not independently verify.
