- Wikipedia, "Strong NP-completeness" — the strong/weak NP-hardness distinction, and why strong
  NP-hardness rules out a pseudo-polynomial algorithm and a fully polynomial-time approximation
  scheme. [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_NP-completeness)
- Wikipedia, "Knapsack problem" — the decision form's NP-completeness and the O(nW)
  pseudo-polynomial dynamic program. [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knapsack_problem)
- Wikipedia, "Bin packing problem" — strong NP-completeness by reduction from 3-partition, and
  the fixed-bin-count / fixed-capacity escapes.
  [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bin_packing_problem)
- Wikipedia, "3-partition problem" — the standard strongly NP-complete example, and the
  bounded-integers / unary-encoding equivalent statement of strong hardness.
  [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-partition_problem)

Named for attribution only, not read directly (per the chapter plan): Garey and Johnson,
"'Strong' NP-completeness results: motivation, examples, and implications" (J. ACM 25(3), 1978)
and *Computers and Intractability* (1979), the origin of the strong/weak NP-hardness
distinction; Backurs and Indyk, "Edit distance cannot be computed in strongly subquadratic time
(unless SETH is false)" (STOC 2015), named once in post-notes as a pointer to fine-grained
complexity.
