- Wikipedia, "Minimum spanning tree" — the cut property and cycle property, stated and proved by those exact names, this topic's primary source for the two properties' own wording. [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_spanning_tree)
- Erickson, *Algorithms* (2019), Chapter 7 "Minimum Spanning Trees" — the "safe edge" lemma, an independent statement of the cut property's own fact under a different name, attributed by Erickson to Prim. [source](https://jeffe.cs.illinois.edu/teaching/algorithms/book/07-mst.pdf)
- SciPy Documentation, `scipy.sparse.csgraph.minimum_spanning_tree` — SciPy's own stated choice of Kruskal's algorithm. [source](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.sparse.csgraph.minimum_spanning_tree.html)
- NetworkX Documentation, `minimum_spanning_tree` — NetworkX's own three algorithm choices (Kruskal, Prim, Borůvka), Kruskal the default. [source](https://networkx.org/documentation/stable/reference/algorithms/generated/networkx.algorithms.tree.mst.minimum_spanning_tree.html)
- J. B. Kruskal, "On the shortest spanning subtree of a graph and the traveling salesman problem," *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* 7(1), 1956, pp. 48–50 — cited bibliographically, confirmed against Crossref, not opened or quoted.
