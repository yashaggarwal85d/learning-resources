### English (Standard German phonology)

**Vowel length and quality (Vowels > Monophthongs, paragraph after the Notes list)**
> "Although there is also a [[Vowel length|length contrast]], vowels are often analyzed according to a [[tenseness]] contrast, with long {{IPA|/iː, yː, uː, eː, øː, oː/}} being the tense vowels and short {{IPA|/ɪ, ʏ, ʊ, ɛ, œ, ɔ/}} their lax counterparts. Like the English [[checked vowel]]s, the German lax vowels require a following consonant, with the notable exception of {{IPA|/ɛː/}} (which is absent in many varieties, as discussed below). {{IPA|/a/}} is sometimes considered the lax counterpart of tense {{IPA|/aː/}} in order to maintain this tense/lax division."

**Vowel length, unstressed and loanword vowels (Vowels > Monophthongs, same paragraph)**
> "Short {{IPA|[i, y, u, e, ø, o]}} occur in unstressed syllables of [[loanwords]], for instance in {{lang|de|Ps'''y'''ch'''o'''m'''e'''trie}} {{IPA|[psyçomeˈtʁiː]}} ('psychometry')."

**Vowel length, unstressed /aː/ (Vowels > Monophthongs, same paragraph)**

**Phonemic status of /ɛː/ (Vowels > Monophthongs > Phonemic status of /ɛː/)**
> "The existence of a phoneme {{IPA|/ɛː/}} in German is disputed.{{sfn|Noack|2023}}"
> "The distinction between the long lax {{IPA|/ɛː/}} and the long tense {{IPA|/eː/}} does not exist in some varieties of Standard German, and many authors treat the {{IPA|/ɛː/}} phoneme as peripheral and regard a distinction between it and {{IPA|/eː/}} as a spelling pronunciation."
> "Most commonly, they are merged before an intervocalic {{IPA|/r/}}, so that potential minimal pairs such as {{lang|de|'''Äh'''re}} 'ear of grain' and {{lang|de|'''Eh'''re}} 'honor' or {{lang|de|B'''ä'''ren}} 'bears' and {{lang|de|B'''ee'''ren}} 'berries' are rendered homophonous, as {{IPA|/ˈɛːrə/}} and {{IPA|/ˈbɛːrən/}}."
> "In contexts other than before intervocalic {{IPA|/r/}}, the contrast between {{IPA|/ɛː/}} and {{IPA|/eː/}} is more stable, so that {{lang|de|b'''ä'''ten}} {{IPA|/ˈbɛːtən/}} 'bid, conjunctive', {{lang|de|D'''ä'''nen}} {{IPA|/ˈdɛːnən/}} 'Danes' and {{lang|de|S'''ä'''gen}} {{IPA|/ˈzɛːɡən/}} ('saws, n.') may be differentiated from {{lang|de|b'''e'''ten}} {{IPA|/ˈbeːtən/}} 'to pray', {{lang|de|d'''eh'''nen}} {{IPA|/ˈdeːnən/}} 'to stretch' and {{lang|de|S'''e'''gen}} {{IPA|/ˈzeːɡən/}} 'blessing'."

**Umlaut forms, vowel length (Sound changes > Sound changes and mergers)**

**Umlaut as a trigger for ich-Laut (Consonants > Ich-Laut and ach-Laut)**

**Ich-Laut (Consonants > Ich-Laut and ach-Laut)**
> "'''{{lang|de|Ich-Laut}}''' is the [[voiceless palatal fricative]] {{IPAblink|ç}} (which is found in the word {{lang|de|ich}} {{IPA|[ɪç]}} 'I'), and '''{{lang|de|ach-Laut}}''' is the [[voiceless velar fricative]] {{IPAblink|x}} (which is found in the word {{lang|de|ach}} {{IPA|[ax]}} the interjection 'oh', 'alas'). {{lang|de|Laut}} {{IPA|[laʊ̯t]}} is the German word for 'sound, [[phone (phonetics)|phone]]'. In German, these two sounds are [[allophone]]s occurring in [[complementary distribution]]. The allophone {{IPAblink|x}} occurs after back vowels and {{IPA|/a aː/}} (for instance in {{lang|de|Buch}} {{IPA|[buːx]}} 'book'), the allophone {{IPAblink|ç}} after front vowels (for instance in {{lang|de|mich}} {{IPA|[mɪç]}} 'me/myself') and consonants (for instance in {{lang|de|Furcht}} {{IPA|[fʊʁçt]}} 'fear', {{lang|de|manchmal}} {{IPA|[ˈmançmaːl]}} 'sometimes')."
> "The diminutive [[suffix]] {{lang|de|-chen}} is always pronounced with an {{lang|de|ich-Laut}} {{IPA|[-çən]}}."
> "According to Kohler,<ref>{{Harvcoltxt|Kohler|1977}} and {{Harvcoltxt|Kohler|1990}}, as cited in {{Harvcoltxt|Wiese|1996|p=210}}</ref> the German {{lang|de|ach-Laut}} is further differentiated into two allophones, {{IPAblink|x}} and {{IPAblink|χ}}: {{IPAblink|x}} occurs after {{IPA|/uː, oː/}} (for instance in {{lang|de|Buch}} {{IPA|[buːx]}} 'book') and {{IPAblink|χ}} after {{IPA|/a, aː/}} (for instance in {{lang|de|Bach}} {{IPA|[baχ]}} 'brook'), while either {{IPAblink|x}} or {{IPAblink|χ}} may occur after {{IPA|/ʊ, ɔ, aʊ̯/}}, with {{IPAblink|χ}} predominating."
> "''ç'' and ''x'' are traditionally regarded as allophones after front vowels and back vowels, respectively." (Consonants, list item; the source text is: "*{{IPAblink|ç}} and {{IPAblink|x}} are traditionally regarded as allophones after front vowels and back vowels, respectively.")

**r (Consonants, /r/ list)**
> "*{{IPA|/r/}} can be uvular, alveolar or even dental, a consonant or a semivowel, see below."
> "*{{IPA|/r/}} has a number of possible realizations:"
> "** Voiced apical coronal trill {{IPA|[{{IPAplink|r|r̺}}]}}"
> "*** Distribution: Common in the south (Bavaria and many parts of Switzerland and Austria), but it is also found in some speakers in central and northern Germany, especially the elderly. [...] Even more common are uvular realizations, fricatives {{IPA|[{{IPAplink|ʁ}} ~ {{IPAplink|χ}}]}} and a trill {{IPAblink|ʀ}}."
> "** Voiced uvular trill {{IPAblink|ʀ}}, [...]"
> "*** Distribution: Occurs in some conservative varieties—most speakers with a uvular {{IPA|/r/}} realize it as a fricative or an approximant."
> "*** {{Harvcoltxt|Mangold|2005}} and {{Harvcoltxt|Kohler|1999}} describe it as a [[voiced uvular fricative]] {{IPA|[ʁ]}};"
> "**** {{Harvcoltxt|Kohler|1999}} writes that \"the place of articulation of the consonant varies from uvular in e.g. {{lang|de|rot}} ('red') to velar in e.g. {{lang|de|treten}} ('kick'), depending on back or front vowel contexts.\""
> "*** Distribution: Almost all areas apart from Bavaria and parts of Switzerland."
> "** Near-open central unrounded vowel {{IPAblink|ɐ}} is a post-vocalic allophone of (mostly dorsal) varieties of {{IPA|/r/}}. The non-syllabic variant of it is not always near-open or central; it is similar to either {{IPAblink|ɑ}} or {{IPAblink|ə}}, depending on the environment."
> "*** Distribution: Widespread, but less common in Switzerland."

**Vocalised /r/ and -er (Vowels > Monophthongs > Notes, /ɐ/; and Diphthongs > Phonetic)**
> "{{IPA|/ɐ/}} is near-open central unrounded {{IPAblink|ɐ}}. [...] It is a common allophone of the sequence {{IPA|/ər/}} common to all German-speaking areas but Switzerland. As schwa {{IPA|/ə/}} is never pronounced here, it is also possible to interpret {{IPAblink|ɐ}} as the vocalic allophone of the syllabic sonorant {{IPA|/r̩/}}."
> "According to a 2020 study, {{IPA|[ɐ]}} is not distinguished from unstressed {{IPA|[a]}} in Northern Standard German. Word pairs such as {{lang|de|Oper}} 'opera' (traditionally transcribed {{IPA|[ˈoːpɐ]}}) and {{lang|de|Opa}} 'grandpa' (traditionally transcribed {{IPA|[ˈoːpa]}}) are thus homophones (as {{IPA|[ˈoːpɐ]}}), rather than minimal pairs."
> "In the varieties where speakers vocalize {{IPA|/r/}} to {{IPAblink|ɐ}} in the syllable coda, a diphthong ending in {{IPA|[ɐ̯]}} may be formed with every stressable vowel:"
> "In dialects with r-vocalization, historical {{IPA|/a(ː)r/}} (phonetically typically a long monophthong {{IPAblink|äː}}) may undergo a shortening akin to {{IPA|/aː/}} when unstressed, as in one pronunciation of {{lang|de|Rad'''ar'''}} 'radar' as {{IPA|[ˈʁaːda]}} (phonemically {{IPA|/ˈraːdaːr/}})."

**Final devoicing (Consonants > Fortis–lenis pairs > Coda devoicing)**
> "In varieties from Northern Germany, lenis stops in the [[syllable coda]] are realized as fortis stops. This does not happen in varieties from Southern Germany, Austria or Switzerland."
> "Fricatives, on the other hand, are acknowledged to be truly and contrastively voiced in Northern Germany, so they can be said to undergo [[Final-obstruent devoicing|coda devoicing]] according to this account as well."
> "It is disputed whether coda devoicing is due to a constraint which specifically operates on syllable codas or whether it arises from constraints which \"protect voicing in privileged positions\"."
> (Note: the term "Auslautverhärtung" does not appear. The article links the concept to the English term "coda devoicing".)

**Glottal stop before initial vowels (Consonants, list item)**
> "In many varieties of standard German, the glottal stop, {{IPAblink|ʔ}}, occurs in careful speech before word stems that begin with a vowel and before stressed vowels word-internally, as in ''Oase'' [ʔo.ʔaː.zə] (twice). It is much more frequent in northern varieties than in the south. It is not usually considered a phoneme. In colloquial and dialectal speech, {{IPA|[ʔ]}} is often omitted, especially when the word beginning with a vowel is unstressed."
> (Note: "Knacklaut" does not appear in the English article.)

**Schwa (Vowels > Monophthongs > Notes, /ə/)**
> "{{IPA|/ə/}} has been variously described as mid central unrounded {{IPAblink|ə}}. [...] It occurs only in unstressed syllables, for instance in {{lang|de|b'''e'''setz'''e'''n}} {{IPA|[bəˈzɛtsən]}} ('occupy')."
> "If a [[sonorant]] follows in the [[syllable coda]], the schwa often disappears so that the sonorant becomes syllabic, for instance {{lang|de|Kiss'''en'''}} {{IPA|[ˈkɪsn̩]}} ('pillow'), {{lang|de|Es'''el'''}} {{IPA|[ˈeːzl̩]}} ('donkey')."

**Aspiration (Consonants, list item; and Consonants > Fortis–lenis pairs)**
> "*The voiceless stops {{IPAslink|p}}, {{IPAslink|t}}, {{IPAslink|k}} are strongly [[Aspiration (phonetics)|aspirated]] at the start of a word or stressed syllable (except before a [[sibilant]]), word-finally before a pause, and in some suffixes; unaspirated before a sibilant in the same word; and weakly aspirated elsewhere."
> "The fortis stops {{IPA|/p, t, k/}} are [[Aspirated consonant|aspirated]] in many varieties. The aspiration is strongest in the onset of a [[stressed syllable]] (such as {{lang|de|Taler}} {{IPA|[ˈtʰaːlɐ]}} 'thaler'), weaker in the [[Onset (linguistics)|onset]] of an [[unstressed syllable]] (such as {{lang|de|Vater}} {{IPA|[ˈfaːtʰɐ]}} 'father'), and weakest in the [[syllable coda]] (such as in {{lang|de|Saat}} {{IPA|[zaːtʰ]}} 'seed')."

### German (Aussprache der deutschen Sprache; source of the redirect from "Deutsche Phonologie")

**Vowel length and quality (Vokalsystem, introduction)**
> "Die Vokalphoneme der betonten Silben werden oft in Paare eingeteilt:" [...] "Zur phonologischen Begründung dieser Paarbildungen gibt es verschiedene Ansätze:"
> "* Das Unterscheidungsmerkmal ist die [[Vokalquantität]]. Der Unterschied in der [[Vokalqualität]] folgt sekundär daraus."
> "Geschlossene (lange) Vokale werden in unbetonter Position meistens kurz ausgesprochen, z.&nbsp;B. /{{IPA|ɡeˈnoːm}}/, /{{IPA|viˈtaːl}}/."
> "''Ritt'' /{{IPA|rɪt}}/ und ''riet'' /{{IPA|riːt}}/ unterscheiden sich beispielsweise auch in der Qualität voneinander, wie die Notation des [[Internationales Phonetisches Alphabet|Internationalen Phonetischen Alphabets]] zeigt. Die Mehrzahl der langen Vokalphoneme werden also geschlossener ausgesprochen und sind andere Phoneme als ihre kurzen verschrifteten Entsprechungen."
> "/{{IPA|ɛː}}/ wie in ''K'''ä'''se'' wird in mehreren Varietäten der deutschen Standardsprache, besonders im Norden Deutschlands und Osten Österreichs, üblicherweise wie /{{IPA|eː}}/ ausgesprochen."

**Vowel length rules (Ausspracheregeln > Vokalbuchstaben > Unterscheidung von Vokalquantität und -qualität bei einzelnen Vokalbuchstaben)**
> "Die deutsche Rechtschreibung bezeichnet die Quantität (Länge) und damit auch die Qualität (geschlossen/offen) der Vokale nur teilweise direkt. Trotzdem kann die Unterscheidung zwischen langen und kurzen resp. geschlossenen und offenen Vokalen und damit die Entscheidung, welches Phonem eines Vokalphonempaares zu wählen ist, meistens aus der Schreibung erschlossen werden."
> "Von daher leitet sich die Regel ab, dass zwei gleiche Konsonantenbuchstaben (ebenso „ck“ und „tz“) nach einem einzelnen Vokal dessen Kürze signalisieren (zum Beispiel in „Sonne“, „irren“, „Ratte“, „Masse“), [...]"
> "Umgekehrt deutet daher ein einzelner Konsonantenbuchstabe (inkl. [[ß]], dessen Gebrauch gerade in dieser funktionalen Abgrenzung zu „ss“ begründet wird) die Länge des vorangehenden Vokals an („Krone“, „hören“, „raten“, „Maße“), da er, wie gesagt, den Vokal in einer offenen Silbe stehen lässt. (Ausnahme: der Konsonantenbuchstabe [[x]] – vor „x“ wird ein einzelner Vokalbuchstabe ''immer'' kurz gesprochen, z.&nbsp;B. „Hexe“, „Axt“.)"
> "Ebenfalls lang sind Vokale, die zwar in geschlossenen Silben stehen, welche aber so erweitert werden können, dass eine offene Silbe entsteht."

**Miete vs Mitte**
> The German article has no contrast of Miete and Mitte. It uses "Mitte" only as an example for short /ɪ/: "| {{IPA-Phonem|ɪ}} || {{Audio|De-Mitte.ogg|M'''i'''tte}} || kurzer, [[fast geschlossener fast vorderer ungerundeter Vokal]] || {{IPA-Phon|ɪ}}" (Vokalsystem > Monophthonge table). "Miete" does not appear.

**Ich-Laut and ach-Laut (Konsonantensystem, table)**
> "| [[stimmloser palataler Frikativ]] (Ich-Laut) – Dieser Laut bildet zusammen mit [x] ein komplementäres [[Allophon]]-Paar. Er tritt nach vorderen Vokalen sowie nach Konsonanten auf. **r (Konsonantensystem, table)**
