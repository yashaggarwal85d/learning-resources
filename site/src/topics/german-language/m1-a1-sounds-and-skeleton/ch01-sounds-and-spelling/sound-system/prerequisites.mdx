This is the first topic in German, and the course starts from nothing: no German is needed. The reel compares German sounds with English ones, and uses a little phonetic notation, which it explains as it goes.

<section class="prereq" data-section="english">

### <span class="n">01</span> You speak English

The reel explains each German sound by contrast with English: the catch in the middle of "uh-oh", the way "an apple" runs together, the long and short vowels of "beat" and "bit". You don't need any phonetics, only your own ear for English.

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> can you hear the catch in the middle of "uh-oh"? (It is the little closing of the throat between the two syllables, the same movement as a very quiet cough.)</p>

<section class="prereq" data-section="ipa-notation">

### <span class="n">02</span> Square-bracket notation (IPA), the basics

The reel explains this on its first step; this is the same in a few lines. [Square brackets] mean *sounds*, not spelling: [ˈmɪtə] is how Mitte is said. ː after a symbol means the sound is long. ˈ before a syllable marks the stressed one. One symbol is one sound, even when the spelling uses two letters (tt is one [t], ch is one [ç]).

<p class="check"><b>Check yourself:</b> how many sounds does [ˈmɪtə] contain? (Four: m, ɪ, t, ə. The stress mark and the doubled tt of the spelling don't add sounds.)</p>
