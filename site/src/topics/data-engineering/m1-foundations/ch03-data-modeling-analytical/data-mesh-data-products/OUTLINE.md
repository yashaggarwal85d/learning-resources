# Outline — de.data-mesh-data-products

13-step plan, no lab of its own. First predict within the first four steps.

1. **Hook.** One central team now owns every pipeline across every domain in the shop. The team
   that understands orders least owns the orders pipeline. Is that still the right shape?
2. **Reveal: domain ownership, quoted directly.** Dehghani's own reversal — domains host and
   serve their own datasets, instead of flowing data into a centrally owned lake (quote 1).
3. **Predict.** If every domain now owns and serves its own data, does that alone make the
   data trustworthy to everyone else who needs it? (within the first four steps)
4. **Reveal: data as a product, named and defined, quoted directly.** The shift from "extracted
   dataset" to "product with customers," and the specific standard it has to meet.
5. **Reveal: the four data-product qualities, quoted directly.** Discoverable (a registry),
   addressable (a unique, programmatic address), trustworthy (an honest quality guarantee), and
   inter-operable (shared standards for correlating across domains) (quotes 2-5). Takeaway: "data
   as a product" is a checklist, not a slogan — a domain either meets all four or it doesn't.
6. **Predict.** Does handing every domain full ownership of its own data pipeline cost nothing
   extra, since each domain was already building its own operational systems anyway?
7. **Reveal: the real cost of decentralizing without a platform, quoted directly.** Dehghani's
   own naming of the duplicated-effort, duplicated-specialized-skill cost every domain pays if
   nothing is shared underneath them (quote 6). Takeaway: domain ownership alone multiplies
   infrastructure cost; it doesn't eliminate it.
8. **Reveal: the four named principles together, quoted directly.** Dehghani's own list, stated
   as collectively necessary and sufficient — no one of the four substitutes for the others
   (quotes 7, 8).
9. **Predict.** If a company adopts domain ownership and data-as-a-product tomorrow, but has no
   shared self-serve platform yet, does the mesh still work?
10. **Reveal: the self-serve platform's own stated reason to exist, quoted directly.** The
    specific gap it closes — specialized infrastructure skill that would otherwise be needed in
    every domain (quote 9 — the same quote as step 7, read as the self-serve platform principle's
    own justification). Takeaway: the platform isn't optional polish; without it, the other
    principles' real cost lands on every domain separately.
11. **Reveal: federated computational governance, quoted directly.** Dehghani's own precise
    definition — domain-local autonomy plus a small set of globally enforced, platform-automated
    rules (quote 10).
12. **Recap.** Domain ownership reverses who hosts data; data as a product is a specific
    four-quality standard, not a slogan; none of this is free without a shared self-serve
    platform underneath it; federated governance is what keeps the resulting mesh interoperable
    rather than fragmented.
13. **Prove it.** A company rolls out domain ownership and data-as-a-product standards across
    every team this quarter, with the shared self-serve platform still eighteen months from
    ready. What actually happens to that mesh in the meantime?
