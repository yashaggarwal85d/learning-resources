- **Corporate Information Factory (Inmon's approach)**: a single, normalized enterprise data model built first, with departmental marts fed from it afterward. *Where you meet it:* "build the warehouse, then the marts" as a stated project order.
- **Enterprise data warehouse bus architecture (Kimball's approach)**: an incremental, business-process-first model, integrated through dimensions shared across every process. *Where you meet it:* shipping one star schema for one business process before anything else is designed.
- **Conformed dimension**: one shared copy of a dimension (customer, product, date) reused by every star schema that needs it, so they all agree without a separate integration model. *Where you meet it:* a single `dim_customer` table referenced by every fact table in the warehouse.
- **Top-down vs. bottom-up**: Inmon's approach starts from the whole enterprise model and works down to marts; Kimball's starts from one business process and works up to enterprise coverage. *Where you meet it:* which gets built and shipped first, the whole model or the first star.
- **ETL vs. ELT**: transforming data before loading it into the warehouse, versus loading it raw and transforming it afterward inside the warehouse itself. *Where you meet it:* whether a pipeline's transform step runs before or after the data lands.
