- **Natural key**: a business attribute (an email address, a national id number) reused as a row's identity. *Where you meet it:* a table keyed on `customer_email` because it looked unique and stable when the table was designed.
- **Surrogate key**: a system-generated identifier with no business meaning, chosen specifically so nothing about the business ever gives it a reason to change. *Where you meet it:* an auto-increment `customer_id` sitting beside the email it was introduced to protect against.
- **Durable key**: a key that stays constant across every version of a row that later gets versioned (as in SCD2), distinct from a surrogate key that can change per version. *Where you meet it:* a dimension table where a customer's current and historical rows all need one thing in common.
- **Sequence**: a database-managed counter that hands out increasing numbers one at a time, one coordinator for the whole table. *Where you meet it:* the default primary-key generator in a single-writer system.
- **UUIDv7**: a time-ordered universally unique identifier, a timestamp in its leading bits and randomness in the rest. *Where you meet it:* a key generated independently by many writers that still sorts close to insertion order.
- **Index locality**: how close together, in index order, values inserted close together in time actually land. *Where you meet it:* whether a B-tree index stays small and dense or grows bloated and scattered as rows arrive.
- **Birthday bound**: the standard formula for how many randomly generated values it takes before a collision becomes likely, given the space they're drawn from. *Where you meet it:* deciding whether a hash key is actually safe at a given scale, or just assumed to be.
