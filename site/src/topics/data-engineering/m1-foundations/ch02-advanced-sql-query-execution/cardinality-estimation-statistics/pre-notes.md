- **Predicate**: a condition in a `WHERE` clause that a row either satisfies or doesn't. *Where you meet it:* every selectivity number in this topic is "the fraction of rows that satisfy one predicate."
- **Selectivity**: the fraction of a table's rows a predicate is expected to match, a number between 0 and 1. *Where you meet it:* every formula here computes a selectivity, then multiplies it by the row count to get an estimate.
- **Estimated vs. actual rows**: the planner's guess, printed before a query runs, versus the real count once `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` has actually run it. *Where you meet it:* step 1's hook is the gap between these two numbers.
- **Statistical independence**: two events don't affect each other's probability, so the chance of both is the product of their separate chances. *Where you meet it:* the planner's independence assumption applies this general rule to `WHERE` clauses — and real, correlated data breaks it.
- **Skew**: some values occurring far more often than others, instead of every value being equally likely. *Where you meet it:* `orders.status`, where `refunded` is rare and five other values each take roughly a sixth of the table.
- **Random sampling**: reading a subset of a table's rows and estimating from that, rather than scanning every row. *Where you meet it:* why PostgreSQL's own documentation calls its statistics "always approximate even when freshly updated."
- **Bulk load**: inserting a large batch of rows in one operation, instead of a steady trickle over time. *Where you meet it:* the whole setup for the stale-statistics predict step.
