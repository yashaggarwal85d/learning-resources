# Fact-check: Memory hierarchy and latency numbers

Checked on 12 October 2026 against the primary sources' own fetched texts in `sources/` (raw `pdftotext` / HTML text, not `SOURCES.md` summaries), plus sources fetched for this check with `node scripts/sources.mjs fetch`. All arithmetic was recomputed in Python 3. Every correction below has been applied to `OUTLINE.md` and `SOURCES.md`. `node scripts/sources.mjs check` passes: 110 quotes found word for word, 0 problems (56 before the check).

Headline changes: (1) the Seagate "Performance seek 3.2 ms" contents line was sound power in bels: corrected. (2) The 168 IOPS now has Seagate's own data sheet on seagate.com as its source. (3) Intel's manual has a newer edition (248966-051, October 2026). The Skylake table is renumbered (2-9 → 2-11) and the Gracemont chapter is now Crestmont. (4) Step 12's "provided each request streams at least 166 MB/s" was necessary but not sufficient: at k = 41 and 166 MB/s a request the run takes about 321 s, not 162 s. (5) Step 7's object-storage inequalities ran the wrong way. (6) Prove it 1's "floor 80 times lower" ignored the 8 KB bytes term: it is 66×. (7) Several smaller wording fixes (HDD physics, "no cloud", queue depth, NUMA balancing).

## Red team: attacks, findings and what changed

| # | Target | Attack | Finding | Change applied |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Step 3 caption | Who says "200 or more"? | Drepper (2007), not Intel. The caption ran it on from "Intel's manual gives…". | Caption now names Drepper. Builder note: ns hold only at 2 GHz (at 3 GHz, 4–6 cycles = 1.3–2 ns). |
| R2 | Step 4 HDD physics | "4.16 ms … before the head even moves" | Wrong order. The head seeks first, then the drive waits on average half a turn (4.17 ms). The manual publishes no seek time; its "Performance seek 3.2/3.4" is sound power in bels. | Caption rewritten; SOURCES contents line corrected; acoustics quoted. |
| R3 | Step 4/7/10 HDD α = 5.95 ms | Is 1/168 s the latency of one read? | No. It is the per-read time at QD16 throughput (reordered). One at a time is slower (Little: 16 queued at 168/s ≈ 95 ms per request in queue). As a floor value it is safe (optimistic). | Band note "168/s with 16 queued"; Deep dive inversion fixed; case B note. |
| R4 | Floor = max(bytes/β, R·α/k) | Overlap vs add | Each term alone is a lower bound: bytes can't move faster than β, and R waits of α over k slots take ≥ Rα/k. So the max is valid. With k = 1 they add; with k slots each busy α + s/b the time is R(α + s/b)/k ≥ max. The max never overstates; it can understate by up to 2×. Dean's 2009 "Design 2" (18 ms) is the additive per-request form. | Overlap check added to step 10 real values; Deep dive already had the closer estimate, now with 52/82. |
| R5 | Step 12 "k = 41 provided each request streams ≥ 166 MB/s" | Plug 41 and 166 MB/s into R(α + s/b)/k | 65,536 × (0.1 + 16,777,216/165.9e6) / 41 = 321.5 s, double the drive. 166 MB/s is the average per-slot share and is necessary, not sufficient. With instant transfers k = 41 is right; at 625 MB/s k = 52 (51.4); at 166 MB/s k = 82 (81.5); even at 12.5 GB/s per request k = 42 (41.08). | Problem paragraph, step 12 readout (second line with 52/82), caption, slider note, Changes #3. |
