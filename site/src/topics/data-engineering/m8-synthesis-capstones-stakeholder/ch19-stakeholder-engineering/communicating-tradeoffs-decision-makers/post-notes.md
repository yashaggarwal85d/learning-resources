<section data-section="outcomes">

## Outcomes

Restate a priced SLO tier's own cost in business rather than engineering terms; name the one
dominant cost lever behind a stated price difference; write a one-page, bottom-line-up-front
executive summary and a separate engineering deep dive recommending the same tier, and state what
belongs in each.

</section>

<section data-section="internals">

## Internals

Amazon's own 2017 letter to shareholders states the practice directly: "We don't do PowerPoint (or
any other slide-oriented) presentations at Amazon. Instead, we write narratively structured
six-page memos. We silently read one at the beginning of each meeting in a kind of 'study hall.'"
This topic's own worked example borrows the shape of that split, a short, conclusion-first
document paired with a longer one, not Amazon's own six-page length or its silent-reading ritual,
which `examples.py` states plainly is a disclosed, classroom-scale exercise rather than a
reproduction of Amazon's own practice.

</section>

<section data-section="lab">

## Lab

A written artifact, not a measured number: this chapter has no system under test (see
CHAPTER.md's own "Engine of record" note). `examples.py` restates topic 1's own three priced tiers
in business terms, computes which single lever (recompute frequency, amplified by the streaming
surcharge) accounts for most of the cost gap to the fast tier, and writes a one-page executive
summary and a longer engineering deep dive that both recommend the identical tier, checked against
a stated length ceiling and a stated list of what each document must include.

</section>

<section data-section="production">

## Production failures this topic warns about

**Treating document length as a style choice rather than a decision about who can act on it.** The
hook's own failure is not that the twelve-page version was wrong; the same numbers, the same
recommendation, failed only because the room that received it had no time to read it.

**Listing every contributing cost factor as equally important.** A one-pager that lists five
factors with no ranking leaves the reader to guess which one actually matters, which is the same
gap examples.py's own dominant-lever computation closes mechanically.

</section>

<section data-section="frontier">

## Frontier

A one-pager that drops its own dominant lever to save space has stopped being a summary and
started being a guess dressed up as one. The frontier isn't writing shorter; it's knowing which
few facts a short document cannot afford to drop.

</section>

<section data-section="where-this-goes-next">

## Where this goes next

A tier gets chosen, and the one-page summary that got it chosen is read once and then forgotten
the moment the meeting ends. The decision itself, which tier, why, and what would change the
team's mind later, still has to survive the person who made it leaving the room, which is the next
topic's own subject.

</section>
