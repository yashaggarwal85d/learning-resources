## Red team

- **Is AWS IoT Greengrass's Stream Manager a fair stand-in for "one real edge-gateway or broker-bridge product," given it's a managed-cloud product rather than a plant-floor appliance?** CHAPTER.md asks only for "the documentation of one real edge-gateway or broker-bridge product selected and verified at research time," with no requirement that it be an on-premises appliance; Greengrass Core runs on customer-owned edge hardware specifically to buffer during WAN loss, which is the exact mechanism this topic teaches, and its own documentation is real, fetchable, and quoted directly rather than from memory.
- **Does widening Sparkplug's own sequence number inside `examples.py`, rather than Sparkplug's wire protocol itself, overstate the fix?** No — this is exactly what CHAPTER.md proposes ("the gateway adds a wider counter," not "Sparkplug's field is widened"); a single implementation cannot change a published protocol's own byte width, so the realistic fix lives in the gateway's own key, not in Sparkplug's frame.
- **Is `build_overflowing_buffer`'s 10-of-120 alarm ratio (roughly one point in twelve) realistic, or chosen to make degrade look artificially good?** It's disclosed in `OUTLINE.md` as a deliberately clean split precisely so the three policies' behavior differs measurably and verifiably; the qualitative claim (priority-first never drops a higher-priority point while age-only policies can) holds at any nonzero alarm ratio, not only this one.
- **Is a constant (non-growing) clock drift in `build_drifted_batch` a realistic model of real clock drift, which normally accumulates over time?** CHAPTER.md's own scope line asks specifically for "recording `t_device`/`t_gateway`/clock-offset-estimate and correction of a drifted batch" at reconnect — a single offset estimate from one batch at one point in time, not a continuous drift-tracking model, which is this topic's actual claim; continuous drift tracking is out of scope and not claimed.
