## Red team

- **Could the load-budget math be gamed to always show a breach, making the "fix" look better than it is?** No: the subscription load (1,440 msgs/sec from the fictional tag mix) is computed from the same per-tag change rates used nowhere else, and it is itself still over the stated 500 msgs/sec budget — the example's own numbers say the half-fix (subscribing) is not enough, which argues against the point the hook first seems to make (poll → subscribe) rather than for it. This was deliberately kept rather than tuned away, because the chapter's real point is "read from a system built to absorb it," not "subscribe instead of poll," and a cleaner-looking subscription number would have undercut that point.
- **Does "a unidirectional gateway... may protect Layer 0, 1, and 2 devices from a cybersecurity event... at Layers 3, 4, or 5" actually support the topic's claim that reads can't backpressure a controller?** No, and the topic does not use it for that — this quote is about a security boundary against an attack, not about load or backpressure; it is cited only for the segmentation vocabulary (unidirectional gateways, which layers they sit between), and the load-budget and buffer claims are kept to `examples.py`'s own computed results, not attributed to NIST.
- **Is naming ISA/IEC 62443 "bibliographically only" actually defensible, or does the topic smuggle in ISA's own content under another citation?** Checked: no sentence in `SOURCES.md`, `OUTLINE.md` or this file attributes any specific definition, level name or rule text to ISA/IEC 62443; every verbatim quote in this topic's files is attributed to NIST SP 800-82r3, a document this builder fetched directly and that carries no equivalent restriction.
- **Does the ownership example (`good_count_definition` stays owned by "plant engineering" even though its analytical copy is owned by "data platform") beg its own conclusion, since the dictionary is hand-written to show exactly that?** Partially — `OWNERSHIP` is a fixture, not a derived result, and the assertion checking it is closer to a worked illustration than an experiment. This is disclosed here rather than presented as a discovered result; the topic's `body`/narration text frames it as "the topic states the ownership of a definition," not as something `examples.py` proves from first principles.
