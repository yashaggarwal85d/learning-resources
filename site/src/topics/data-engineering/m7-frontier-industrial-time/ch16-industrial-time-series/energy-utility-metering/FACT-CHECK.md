## Red team

- **Predict step 3** (does a negative difference always mean the same thing?): the tempting wrong answer is "yes, it's always a reset, since that's the most common cause." Strengthened the explain text to name all three causes explicitly and state that only the rollover can be corrected by arithmetic alone — a reset needs bounding, and a swap needs splitting, neither of which "correct" the number the way a rollover's known width does.
- **Counter-example tried against the rollover/reset classifier**: could a rollover's corrected value coincidentally fall inside the reset's "near zero" range, or vice versa, for some input? The classifier in `examples.py` only checks the rollover-corrected value against a plausible consumption band; it never inspects the raw next-reading's proximity to zero directly. Traced a hand case where `next_reading` is deliberately near zero but the rollover-corrected value still lands outside the plausible band (as in the reset example used): the classifier correctly falls through to "reset" because the *corrected* value, not the raw one, is implausible. The two paths don't overlap by construction, since a width-based correction and a near-zero raw reading produce very different corrected magnitudes except in a coincidental edge case this topic doesn't claim to resolve (logged as a known limitation of a purely value-based classifier, which is why CHAPTER.md's own note that a swap needs install-history metadata, not arithmetic, stands).
- **Checked the "previous fix was worse" claim for fairness**: is "reports zero" strictly worse than "reports a bound" in every case, or only when a real reset actually occurred? The claim is scoped correctly in `examples.py` — the comparison is only drawn for the specific case where a reset is confirmed to have happened (the classifier returned "reset"), not as a blanket claim that bounding is always better than zero in every circumstance.
