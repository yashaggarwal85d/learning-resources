## Red team

- **Predict step 4** (does a boundary split ever change a number?): the tempting wrong answer is "no, because the total downtime for the day is identical either way." This is true of the *daily* total but false of *each shift's own* reported number, which is exactly what the question asks about — strengthened the explain text to distinguish the invariant total from the per-shift figures that do change.
- **Counter-example tried against the double-count fix**: could `merge_intervals` silently under-count a case with three or more overlapping intervals, not just two? Traced the merge loop by hand for a three-interval case (A overlaps B, B overlaps C, A does not overlap C): the running `merged[-1]` comparison correctly extends through the chain, since each new interval is compared against the current merged tail, not the original first interval. The two-interval case in `examples.py` is the minimal reproduction, not a special case the algorithm only happens to get right.
- **Checked the MTBF censoring claim against a literal re-read of the Wikipedia source**: the source doesn't use the word "censored" or discuss window-edge truncation directly; this topic's own censoring treatment (excluding the tail after the last observed failure) is standard reliability-engineering practice, applied as course material, not asserted as a quoted claim from the source. Logged here so a later topic doesn't mistake it for a verbatim claim.
- **Checked against topic 3's content** (quality data and SPC): no overlap — topic 3's quality facts and this topic's machine-state/downtime facts are a different grain (inspection-level vs. state-interval-level), and OEE's own Quality factor (good count / total count) is never conflated with topic 3's control-chart or Cp/Cpk material anywhere in this topic's reel or claims.
