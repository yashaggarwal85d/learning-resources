## Red team

- **Is quoting Bristol's own patent instead of his 1990 ISA conference paper a fair substitution, or does it overstate what was verified?** It's disclosed directly in `SOURCES.md` and `OUTLINE.md` rather than silently treated as the original paper: the patent (1987) describes the identical door/pivot/slope mechanism in the same inventor's own words, predates the conference paper by three years, and is the only full-text primary source for this algorithm this research could actually fetch.
- **Does the lopsided signal built specifically to bias the recorded-knee average overstate the claim, compared to a signal built only for the compression-ratio test?** No — CHAPTER.md's own hook describes exactly this shape (a long flat period with few points, a short excursion with many), and `OUTLINE.md` discloses that the main (smooth-ramp) signal alone didn't bias the average enough to demonstrate the claim, so a second signal shaped to match the hook was built rather than loosening the assertion's threshold.
- **Does picking ExcMax = 400 samples arbitrarily understate or overstate exception filtering's own point reduction?** The AVEVA deck names ExMax as "max time (in seconds) allowed between events" without a recommended default value; 400 (out of 3600 total samples, roughly one-ninth of the series) is a chosen, stated constant disclosed in the code's own docstring, not a documented AVEVA recommendation, and does not change the topic's qualitative claims (point reduction stays dominated by the deadband, not by ExcMax firing).
- **Is the step-signal demonstration a real risk this topic claims, or a contrived edge case?** It's CHAPTER.md's own explicit clause ("for a step signal interpolation must be a step, and compression must be configured accordingly"), and a valve position or any other discrete-state tag is exactly this shape in a real plant.
