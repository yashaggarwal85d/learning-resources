- **Pseudonymisation**: processing personal data so it can no longer be attributed to a data subject without additional information, kept separately and protected (GDPR Art. 4(5)). *Where you meet it:* the Hook and the Reveal that it protects data without changing what it legally is.
- **Anonymisation**: processing that removes the realistic possibility of attributing data to a person at all, by anyone. *Where you meet it:* the question the whole reel is asking — was the hashed table ever actually anonymous?
- **Personal data (GDPR Art. 4(1))**: information relating to an identifiable natural person, judged by all the means reasonably likely to be used. *Where you meet it:* the key-holder reveal and the EDPS v SRB elaboration.
- **Plain (unkeyed) cryptographic hash**: a deterministic, public one-way function — same input always gives the same output, and anyone can run it. *Where you meet it:* the dictionary-attack predict and reveal.
- **Keyed hash / HMAC**: a hash that folds in a secret key, so the output isn't reproducible by anyone without that key. *Where you meet it:* "Elaborate: a keyed hash needs a secret the attacker never has."
- **Dictionary attack**: hashing a list of known or guessed candidate values and matching the outputs against a pseudonymised table. *Where you meet it:* the second step's predict/reveal pair.
- **Quasi-identifier**: an attribute (postcode, birth year, product category) that isn't a direct identifier alone but can combine with others to be unique to one person. *Where you meet it:* the linkage-attack predict and reveal.
- **Linkage attack**: joining a dataset against an outside list sharing quasi-identifiers to re-identify individuals without ever touching a key or a dictionary. *Where you meet it:* "Reveal: a real, measurable fraction gets re-identified."
- **Relative identifiability (EDPS v SRB, C-413/23 P)**: whether data is personal data can depend on the recipient's own actual means of re-identification, not a single fixed fact about the dataset. *Where you meet it:* the CJEU elaboration step.
- **Digital Omnibus proposal**: a pending EU reform that would write relative identifiability directly into Article 4(1) and let the Commission specify the criteria by implementing act. *Where you meet it:* the final reveal before the recap.
