# Sources: Quantile sketches: t-digest, DDSketch, KLL (`de.quantile-sketches-t-digest`)

The t-digest paper (Dunning & Ertl), the DDSketch paper (Masson, Rim, Lee), and the KLL sketch
paper (Karnin, Lang, Liberty) are all fetched directly from their authors' own hosted PDFs.
ClickHouse's own documentation names a real warehouse engine's own t-digest function directly,
and the DDSketch paper itself names Datadog's own real, wide-scale use of DDSketch.

## Computing Extremely Accurate Quantiles Using t-Digests (Dunning & Ertl)

URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.04023
Version: 2019

> A t-digest is generated by clustering real-valued samples and retaining the mean and number of samples for each cluster. This clustering can then be used to estimate quantile-related
statistics with particularly high accuracy near the tails of a distribution.

> One version keeps
a buffer of incoming samples. When the buffer fills, the contents are sorted and merged
with the centroids computed from previous samples. This merging form of the t-digest
algorithm has the virtue of allowing all memory structures to be allocated statically.

Contents: t-digest's own clustering mechanism (real-valued samples grouped into clusters by
mean and count) and its own buffer-and-merge construction, which is what makes merging two
digests together a real, supported operation rather than an afterthought.

## DDSketch: A Fast and Fully-Mergeable Quantile Sketch with Relative-Error Guarantees (Masson, Rim, Lee)

URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10693
Version: PVLDB 12(12), 2019

> another crucial property for quantile sketches is mergeablility, i.e.,
several combined sketches must be as accurate as a single sketch of
the same data. We present the first fully-mergeable, relative-error
quantile sketching algorithm with formal guarantees. The sketch is
extremely fast and accurate, and is currently being used by Datadog
at a wide-scale.

> Unfortunately, for data sets with heavy tails, rank-error guarantees can return values with large relative errors. Consider again the
histogram of 2M request response times in Figure 3. If we have a
quantile sketch with a rank accuracy of 0.005, and ask for the 99th
percentile, we are guaranteed to get a value between the 98.5th and
99.5th percentile. In this case this is anywhere from 2 to 20 seconds,
which from an end-user's perspective is the difference between an
annoying delay and giving up on the request.

Contents: the real difference between a rank-error and a relative-error guarantee, with a
concrete worked example of a rank-error bound translating into a useless swing in actual seconds
— and the paper's own statement that DDSketch is "currently being used by Datadog at a
wide-scale," the real engine-naming fact this topic's own scope line asks for.

## Optimal Quantile Approximation in Streams (Karnin, Lang, Liberty)

URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.05346
Version: FOCS 2016

> This paper resolves one of the longest standing basic problems in the streaming computational model. Namely, optimal construction of quantile sketches. An ε approximate quantile
sketch receives a stream of items x1 , . . . , xn and allows one to approximate the rank of any query
up to additive error εn with probability at least 1 − δ.

Contents: KLL's own rank-error guarantee, defined as an additive bound on a query's rank —
the rank-error half of the "rank-error vs relative-error guarantees" clause this topic's own
scope line names, contrasted directly with DDSketch's own relative-error guarantee above.

## quantileTDigest (ClickHouse documentation)

URL: https://clickhouse.com/docs/reference/functions/aggregate-functions/quantileTDigest
Version: retrieved 2026-10-31

> Computes an approximate quantile of a numeric data sequence using the t-digest algorithm.

> The performance of the function is lower than performance of quantile or quantileTiming. In terms of the ratio of State size to precision, this function is much better than quantile.

Contents: a real, shipped warehouse engine's own t-digest-backed percentile function, the
"which engines and observability tools use which" half of this topic's own scope line, naming
t-digest directly rather than DDSketch or KLL.
