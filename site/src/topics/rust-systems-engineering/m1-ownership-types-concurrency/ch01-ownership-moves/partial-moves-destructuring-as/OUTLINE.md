## Learning outcomes ("After this topic you can")

1. **Move a field out of a struct and state exactly what is dropped at scope end, and when**: the moved field dies with its new owner; the struct drops only its still-initialized fields, in declaration order, at its own scope end; a field moved on only one path gets its own drop flag; a refilled field makes the struct whole again; `*b` moves out of a `Box`.
2. **Explain E0509 and E0507 and fix each without cloning**: a type with `Drop` is a single unit because `Drop::drop` gets `&mut self` and may read every field; a borrower may never leave a hole. Fix with an `Option` field and `take`, with `mem::take`/`mem::replace`/`mem::swap`/`Option::replace`, or with an owning removal (`pop`, `swap_remove`, `drain`), and know what each costs (measured: 0 allocations; and the capacity trap of `mem::take` on a pooled `Vec`).
3. **Write a RAII guard that hands its contents back**: `PooledBuf` returns its `Buf` to the `Pool` in `Drop` via `Option::take`, hands it out with `into_inner(mut self)`, and keeps the pool whole on the `?` error path (measured: 100 allocations for 100 errors by hand, 1 with the guard).
4. **Predict whether each binding in a pattern moves, copies or borrows**, including default binding modes under match ergonomics (`&frame` binds `&Header`/`&Buf`), `_` versus `_b`, struct update syntax, and Rust 2024's three reservations (`mut`, `ref`/`ref mut`, `&`/`&mut` under implicit borrowing), and migrate a 2021 pattern that silently copied.

Deinitialization and reinitialization (Reference; step 5). `Copy` fields are copied, so a partial move "applies only if the type cannot be copied" (Reference); a type is `Copy` only if all its components are (std `Copy`).
