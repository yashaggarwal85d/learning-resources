**13. Step 9 explains the central rule as a checker limitation ("does not track a hole through a reference"), not by its real reason** (major)
- *Where:* step 9 caption ("…even though the next line refills it: the borrow checker does not track a hole through a reference"); narration step 9 ("…is refused, even if you'd refill it on the very next line").
- *What:* A sceptical reader hears "the checker isn't smart enough yet". The actual reason is semantic and permanent: between the move and the refill, anything that can panic (the code that computes the refill, a `Drop`, an allocation) would unwind into the owner while its place is empty, and the owner would then read or drop a hole. Through a reference, the type has no state for "temporarily empty", so the only safe forms are the ones that swap in a single indivisible step: which is exactly why `take`/`replace`/`swap` exist and why the crates that allow move-compute-refill abort on panic. The Deep dive has half of this (finding 10); the reel, where most learners stop, has none.
