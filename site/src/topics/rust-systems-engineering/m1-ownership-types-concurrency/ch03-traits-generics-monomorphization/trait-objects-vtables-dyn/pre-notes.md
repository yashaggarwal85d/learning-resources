- **Trait object**: an opaque value behind a pointer (`&dyn Trait`, `Box<dyn Trait>`) that implements a trait, with the concrete type erased. *Where you meet it:* `Box<dyn Codec>` holding either of two different codecs.
- **Fat pointer**: a pointer that is two words wide instead of one. *Where you meet it:* `&dyn Codec` is twice the size of `&A`.
- **Vtable**: a table of function pointers, one per method the trait object's concrete type implements, built once per concrete type. *Where you meet it:* the second word of every `&dyn Codec` or `Box<dyn Codec>`.
- **Late binding**: resolving which function to call at run time, by loading its address out of a vtable, instead of at compile time. *Where you meet it:* calling `.encode()` on a `&dyn Codec`.
- **Lost inlining**: the optimizer's inability to inline a call whose target isn't known until run time — the real dominant cost of `dyn Trait`, more than the indirect call itself. *Where you meet it:* a dyn call that still shows up as a real call in optimized compiler output, where the same call through a known type disappears entirely.
- **Dyn compatibility** (formerly "object safety"): the rules deciding whether a trait can be used as `dyn Trait` at all. *Where you meet it:* a method taking `Self` by value, which blocks the whole trait from being made into a trait object.
- **`where Self: Sized` (the escape hatch)**: a bound on one method that excludes just that method from the vtable, instead of disqualifying the entire trait. *Where you meet it:* fixing the by-value-`Self` method from the previous bullet.
- **Trait upcasting**: converting `&dyn SubTrait` to `&dyn SuperTrait` directly, without going back through the concrete type (stable since Rust 1.86). *Where you meet it:* `let s: &dyn Super = sub_ref;`.
- **Enum dispatch**: a closed `enum` of known implementors, matched explicitly, with no vtable and no fat pointer — the alternative to `dyn Trait` when every type is known in advance. *Where you meet it:* `enum CodecKind { A(A), B(B) }`.
